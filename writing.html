Chapter One

Night. 2 A.M.

The suburban street was empty of life and sound as a black cargo delivery van pulled up to the curb.

Inside the back of the van, Simar put on his backpack and weaved through stacks of packages towards the front partition.

“Five minutes,” a voice from the other side.

Simar peered past the open door. “Five?”

Francis was lying face-up in the driver’s seat, his face hidden beneath a black balaclava and smart shield glasses. “Schedule’s changed . . . “ He pointed at a flatscreen on top of the dashboard—live POV feed from a wheeled delivery robot charging in the back. “Bro’s got a drop to make.”

“Seriously?” Simar shook his head and in a lower tone, mostly to himself said, “Whose bright idea was it to follow delivery schedules—”

Francis turned his head towards him. Simar could feel his judging eyes underneath the black opaque lens. “Stop complaining. You’re the one trying to level up.” He stared at Simar while he pressed a button on the dashboard. The back side door slid open.

“I’m not complaining. Some breathing room would have been nice.”

“You got thirty seconds extra. How’s that for breathing room?”

Simar snorted. “Beautiful.”

Francis sprang up, grabbed Simar’s shoulder and led him to the open door. “Forget about the money, just stick to the protocol.” He leaned closer to him. “If you wanna start designing your own missions, you have to be more serious. The guys above don’t mess around.”

Simar pulled up his mask so Francis could see his face and smiled wide. “I’m totally serious.” He pulled his mask back down, patted his pockets making sure everything was in its place, and hopped out.

As the van took off on auto drive, Simar glanced back at Francis, who was shaking his head as he disappeared behind some boxes.

A slight breeze ruffled the blooming Cherry Blossoms lining both sides of the street. Simar hopped onto the sidewalk and tugged on his rubber gloves. He was dressed in all black; balaclava, a skull like face mask with built in microphone and earbuds, a hoodie, relaxed fit cargo pants and steel toed runners.

It was his uniform. And he wore it proud.

He tapped the side of his glasses. A translucent timer bloomed over on the top-left corner of his vision: 5:09 . . . On the right, an antenna icon glowed faint red. Comms offline. By design. There were to be no communication fingerprints for this task. These weren’t some off the shelf mixed reality headsets. They were custom built from the ground up. No state backdoor. No corporate fingerprints. Not in the code. Not in the chips. He was a ghost to the digital and physical world, roaming around undetected by its autonomous surveillance agents.

Simar cut across the lawn of the target house, a simple two story cookie cutter box. No security cameras to worry about except for the house across and another to the right. But it was the middle of the night, on a weekday, in a good-old fashioned suburban neighbourhood. They were mimicking a typical delivery drop and pickup. All intel provided in advance and movements pre mediated. The client would be asleep and the kitchen door unlocked.

Walk in. Extract. Walk out.

Maybe exchange a few pleasantries, nothing fancy.

No one would even notice. The kids were asleep. The parents were asleep. Hell, even all the dogs seemed to be asleep.

The back house light turned on as he rounded the corner and stepped towards the patio. Bright white light emanated from inside the kitchen. The glass sliding door was fully open. No screen.

Simar’s heart rate jumped up a few ticks as he slipped inside. He exhaled and slid the door shut behind him.

A shadow caught his eye to the left side of the dining area.

At the far end of the antique wooden dinner table, a chair was pulled out and occupied.

A thin green box appeared around the woman’s face with text above her head.

Rita Grover

Birthdate: 03/08/2114

Birthplace: West Lockheed, Cascadia

Target Confirmed.

Confirmation Date: 03/25/2310

02:06

Simar waved away the virtual box. So much for the hundred percent certainty she’d be asleep in her bed. What the hell was she doing in the kitchen? Whose dumbass intel was he relying on?

The woman was slumped to the side, arms folded and legs kicked up on the table. Her jet black hair partially covered her face and ran down her navy blue night gown. To the untrained eye she might appear middle aged—at the peak of her fertile years. Her skin was mostly soft and supple but a few patches of dark wrinkly freckles had made their way through. The dam of eternal youth had cracked and old age was gushing out with vengeance.

Good. Everything about her was unnatural and unfair. He wondered if his father would have ended up the same fate had he been selected like her.

Simar shook his head and slid off his backpack, pulling out a black spray can from one side and a folded cardboard piece from the other. Then came out a Faraday case, tablet and a smartphone—all placed in a row on the table.

He unfolded the cardboard piece, dropped it to the floor and yanked the center handle. It creaked, unfolding into a crate.

The woman didn’t flinch. She was out cold. On drugs, perhaps?

His eyes locked onto her phone, on the tabletop next to her legs. Perfect. Everything he needed was right here. He could just crate her right now and wait for Francis. That was the plan.

It was just too easy though. That minute or two saved from going upstairs, why not do a little bit more? Have a little fun? He’d be waiting for the van anyway. And there were no rules strictly forbidding him from doing so. Omission did not mean prohibition—that’s what he told himself. As long as he got it done on time and didn’t miss his ride, all would be good. He’d get the points he needed, maybe level up ranks, and wait for his next assignment. Pretty soon he’d be designing his own mission protocols.

It wasn’t overconfidence, It was competence.

He plugged her phone into the tablet, followed by his own. Then glanced at the timer. 4:15 . . .

The tablet screen came alive with an hourglass spinner.

Three to four minutes. That was the time needed to jailbreak and take full control of her phone. If only she had kept her software updated and not clicked on a video from her therapist, her story would have turned out quite different. But what could she do? She was loaded and ignorant—a perfect combination. All that extra off-grid currency, embedded in a chip inside her forearm, just there for the taking.

A perfect opportunity for a little wealth transfer.

Simar cleared his throat.

The woman stirred, a little twitch.

Simar leaned back on the table and placed a foot on her seat handle, pushed the seat back.

The woman jerked up in her seat—legs coming off the table. She yelped and moved as far back as she could. “What the fu—”

Simar placed a finger over his mouth. “Shhh, it’s alright, you’re not in any trouble.”

She glanced at the items on the table and then her phone, well out of arms reach. Rubbing her chest, she took a deep breath. “Thought I was dead, had me excited for a sec.” She folded her arms and studied Simar. “What are you supposed to be?” Her voice had a breezy confidence, like he’d interrupted her coffee break.

Simar’s arm twitched. He felt simultaneously offended and astonished. Where was the fear? The scream? Panic? It was just calm detachment wrapped in sarcasm. How was he supposed to entertain her now? Would his script just be as effective?

He drew a slow breath, then planted himself in front of her and bowed. “I’m just a simple messenger to bestow you with a gift.” He took out a golden business card from his hoodie pocket and held it in front of her. She squinted trying to read the letters on the front.

F.L.E.A

“What is that? Flea?” The woman said, raising an eyebrow.

Simar placed a foot on her seat and leaned forward, folding his arms over his knee. He cleared his throat and began reading from the back of the card like it was scripture.

“Are you tired of life? Do you regret becoming a Lifer? Have you ever considered suicide? Do you wish you were dead but too much of a pussy to do it yourself?” He paused. “These are some hard questions, but you don’t have to think about them anymore because—“ He snapped his other finger and pointed at her. “—Because you . . . You are special. On behalf of the Forced Lifer Expiration Association, you have been selected as a lucky winner. Your meaningless and useless existence will finally have a purpose.”

The woman stared at him wide eyed, her jaw ready to drop to the floor. “Is this some kind of sick joke? You a comedic robber or something?”

Simar pocketed the card and stood up. “The organization I’m devoted to is very serious.”

She laughed. “Is that why you call it flea?”

Simar hesitated. “It seems you’re interested in killing yourself.”

“Says who?”

“Your therapy sessions. You can thank your therapist for his atrocious security practices.” He sat back on the table behind him.

Her carefree demeanour faded and Simar saw a flash of fear and shock. She then smiled. “Is that so?”

Simar leaned in. “I have a little bet with my friend and I’d really appreciate if you’d answer honestly. Do you regret the life you’ve lived?”

She thought about it for a second, then scoffed at him. “I’m not giving you the satisfaction of an answer.”

Simar jumped down and walked behind her chair, placed both hands on the headrest. The woman leaned forward, turned her head enough to keep him in her vision.

“Why didn’t you just take the pill? Assisted suicide, it’s what the government prefers. Saves everyone tax dollars” He moved his head to her other side, lowered his voice. “Why go through all this suffering? How many different cancers do you have? And the medical bills—sheesh.”

“Looks like you know everything about me. So why these questions?” She looked up at him, disappointed.

“You’re scared to die, aren’t you? Can’t pull the trigger, even with a pill.”

She didn’t say anything and then looked away. “Answering you won’t change my outcome, will it?

Simar grumbled. What was even the point? For a second, he wondered why he even bothered with the script. She refused to play along to his little soliloquy. He wanted to understand her life choices, guzzle up her regret and fear. But how could she offer any? Only the living could offer it up and it seemed this woman had given up on life a while ago.

The tablet beeped.

Before she could react, Simar snatched his backpack and pulled out a sleek black pouch, shaped like a blood pressure monitor. He ripped it open and grabbed her left arm.

She stared at him in disbelief.

The cuff hissed as it uncoiled, then snapped tight around her forearm.

“Don’t worry,” Simar said. “I’m not gonna kill you. And neither are you.”

She flinched as the cuff tightened, then let out a sharp cry. “What the hell are you doing to me?”

“A little tax cut,” Simar said, tightening his grip as he hooked his arm around her neck and pulled her back into the seat. He leaned closer to her. “I just don’t understand. You want to die and at the same time, you don’t want to die.”

The tablet beeped twice. The cuff hissed and expanded in a split second.

Rita yelped and jerked her arm, stared at the device.

Simar turned her arm and examined the cuff. Three cylindrical chambers, 100ml each, filled with clear liquids, latched underneath the band. A single line threaded from the base of the cuff, pulsing directly into her veins.

Rita blinked repeatedly, staring at the chambers. “This won’t work on—“

Simar dropped her arm, pointed at her wrist. “You got the M269 Exon chip, with HRV based authentication . . . “ He leaned over the table and grabbed the tablet. “Your chip’s under my control now.”

She shivered and rubbed her left shoulder. “That’s impossible—” Her eyes, bloodshot and wet, suddenly opened wide. “You’ll have to keep me alive for this . . .”

There it was.

The fear of God in her eyes.

0:35 . . . The timer turned red.

Simar ignored it and felt a rush of excitement erupt inside as she stared at him in a cold helpless panic. She’d mocked death her whole prolonged life, but now the tables had turned. Now she tasted the exact same fear—life taken, not by choice but divine will. God was on hiatus, so F.L.E.A. picked up that slack. Her life was out of her hands.

The timer beeped again and Simar scrambled over the table and unplugged the phones, placed them inside the faraday box. Then he packed everything in his bag.

The woman watched him on, paralyzed with her thoughts.

He flung his bag over his shoulder and turned to her “Now if you had to pick between suicide and being kept alive like a zombie slave, which would it be?”

“Please no, why are you doing this to me?” She got up from her seat, too weak to run or put up a fight.

Simar grabbed the spray can, shook it and sprayed over her mouth and nose. “I’m just following orders ma’am.”

“Please . . . please, just kill me here now. Do what you want with my money.” Tears rolled down her eyes.

Simar grabbed her arm and pulled her towards crate box. She tried planting her feet, but wobbled around, drowsy. Simar took a step forward and banged his knee on the table leg.

0:20 . . .

Oh shit, Simar thought. He’d taken way too long.

“Please . . .”

Simar grabbed her by the hips, flung her over his shoulder and grimaced in pain as he knee almost buckled.

0:15 . . .

How was he gonna drag her out in time now?

The kitchen door slid open, and Simar trying to balance himself, glanced towards the door.

It was Bro, the delivery robot.

It rolled on inside and lifted up the box at chest height. “You’re getting late—“

Simar rolled the woman inside and exhaled. “I was just getting there.”

The robot spun around and whirled past him out onto the patio and out of view.

Simar ran right after, slamming the door shut behind him.

The cargo van was pulled up with the door open. Bro was waiting inside, holding up the box. Francis was looking on from behind the partition door. His hands were up in the air.

The van began moving.

Simar sprinted full speed, ignoring the bruise on his shin. And as the van pulled away from the curb, he jumped inside, straight into a pile of boxes.

The door slid closed behind him.

“You just had to dick around,” Francis said. “You would have missed the van if I didn’t send him.”

“Who . . . me?” Simar said, catching his breath on his back. A package rolled across his chest.

“Who else?”

Simar scrambled around and pushed himself up. “Yeah . . . Thanks.” He caught his breath as he rummaged the bag for his phone. “It was worth it though.”

“How much?”

Simar stared at his phone and smiled wide. “Two million.”

Francis nodded and glanced at the crate. “Is she even inside?”

Simar motioned to Bro and it dropped the box to the floor. He kicked the lid open. “She was sleeping in the kitchen. Could have thrown the whole thing off.”

“Huh.” Francis took a quick look at her and turned to Simar, shrugged. “Shit happens.”

Simar took off his backpack and hung it on a wall hook. “You should have seen her face. Before she was scared of dying and now she’s scared of staying alive. Would you believe that? I don’t understand these people.”

“Who gives a shit what she thinks.” Francis stretched his arms and pointed at the boxes. “You’re cleaning up this mess.” He then made his way to the front side. “I’d take a nap if I were you. You’re gonna need it.”

Simar pulled off his headgear and hoodie, then sank into the boxes around him. He sprawled out like a king on a throne, arms and legs spread wide, letting the cool air stroke his sweaty skin. He inhaled deeply—euphoric.

A job well done.

If only he levelled up sooner. How many more like her would he need to round up before they gave him more important tasks? How could he prove himself useful faster?

He glanced over at the woman. She was curled on her side, asleep. There were more like her out there—some sad, others pretending to be happy. It didn’t matter, their day was coming too.

Death was inevitable.

✦ ✦ ✦

The van stopped at a gate in front of a large lakeside warehouse.

Simar fumbled over packages as he put on his now dry gear. Groggy and lightheaded, he couldn’t tell if he’d slept too much or too little. The dark cloudy skies didn’t help either.

He took a deep breath and scrambled into the front seat.

Francis glanced at him, nodded and focused up ahead. The steering wheel was flipped inwards with auto drive enabled. Though he had no control over the vehicle, Francis sat up straight and attentive, ready to take over any second.

There was a rattle and the warehouse shutter door began rolling upwards.

The van rolled to a stop at the centre of the warehouse. It was empty and bare—Off-white walls, grey pavement, and enough space to fill a hundred vans.

A black sedan glistened under the bright white lights in front of them.

They got out together and carried the box with the woman inside into the open trunk of the Sedan. The back seats were taken out and replaced with a divider, blocking out access to the front.

Francis hit the close button.

Simar glanced at the cold metal trunk. It was spotless and shiny— as if it had been scrubbed down to the bones.

Simar took one last look at her face. Rita. If only he could see her final moments, if she’d laugh or cry in front of death. It was gonna happen soon. A day or two, and then a one way ticket to an unmarked grave. That’s what he had heard, at least. Anonymous echoes bouncing through encrypted forums and disappearing chat threads. Rumours, whispers, gossip and the truth all twisted together like a rubber band ball, wrapped in a whole lot of static and paranoia.

But beneath it all was something real. A pulse. A shared vision of the future, reverberating around the globe.

Simar wanted in.

Not into the noise of the rubbery ball, but into the invisible symphony at the centre. The one radiating the waves of revolution.

Score enough points, complete enough missions, and he’d rise through the ranks. Scrub. Apprentice. Master.

Grandmaster.

The title wasn’t just status—it was access. Not only would he get a seat on stage at the symphony, it opened the door to the conductor, the orchestrator of the chaos himself.

Some said he wasn’t even real. Just a dead man’s code running on decentralized networks, issuing automated contracts and global directives like notes on a page—guiding operatives for decades and assigning out tasks in real time from the shadows.

A ghost within ghosts.

There was just one name assigned to the conductor.

Always whispered, never confirmed.

Ferez Shotani.

Was he real?

Simar believed he was. And he couldn’t wait to one day be in the same room as him.

He came out of his reverie and turned to Francis. “What should we get on the way back? I’m starving.”

Francis smacked the button again but the trunk door didn’t budge. “Stupid door—“ His glasses pinged. He stood still for a few seconds and turned to Simar.

“What is it?”

“You ever been to a slaughterhouse?”

“What?”

Francis thought about it then took his hand off the trunk. “Are you sure you want to level up?” His voice dropped, eyes flicked to the trunk. “Think very carefully. It’s serious stuff.”

“There’s nothing else I’d rather do.” Simar stared at him. Why was Francis changing his tone? Didn’t he want him to succeed?

Francis hesitated then nodded. “Alright, just making sure.”

Simar stepped forward. “Is that what your message said? Am I actually in?” Excitement bubbled up inside him. This had to be good news. Francis wouldn’t have brought this up for nothing.

“Get your phone out.“

“Hold up—” Simar ran back in the van and powered on his phone. An encrypted message from the Lib3rator app lay on top of his feed.

Excited and nervous, he clicked on the message and typed in his twenty five character long password and waited impatiently as the app loaded.

Get in the SUV.

What? What the hell was that supposed to mean?

He glanced up at Francis who was busy inspecting the spare tire compartment.

Simar swiped back and checked on his current mission progress. All objectives were check-marked green. No mistakes nor warnings. Twenty five thousand deposited and another twenty large pending on the SUV’s arrival to its final destination.

His score was up to 12200 but his ranking was still the same. Grunt.

He didn’t even know how many points were needed to reach Master—forget Grandmaster, if that was even possible.

Disappointed, he pocketed his phone and let out a deep sigh.

Was he stuck at the bottom—just another low-level grunt doing menial jobs with a bunch of faceless nobodies?

Or was it worse than he thought?

He had screwed up. Why else would he go in the SUV with her?

“What’s the matter?” Francis said, turning his head. “You’re not excited?” He got out of the trunk.

Simar approached him. “Just says ‘Get in the SUV.’” He stared at the woman. “Why would they tell me to get in with her? Did I do something stupid—” Maybe he shouldn’t have taken that money.

Francis laughed. “What do you think was gonna happen?”

Simar’s stomach curled inward. “They’re gonna kill me for taking the money?”

“What? No.” Francis laughed. “Get in the SUV means you’re about to be tested.”

“Tested for what? It doesn’t say shit about that.”

“Of course it doesn’t say shit. It’s supposed to come out of the blue, that’s how secrecy works. You thought it would be some joke?”

Simar shrugged. “I thought it happened automatically. Level up, boom. Fireworks.”

“Congratulations. You’ve officially been selected to level up.”

Simar stared at him. “Why didn’t you tell me earlier?”

“I didn’t think it would happen this fast.” Francis rubbed his forehead. “You’re jumping levels.”

“Shit.”

“Yeah. Shit.” Francis exhaled. “You can still say no. We can operate how we normally do.” He swung his arm backwards and punched the close button. The lift gate began to lower with a soft hum.

“So it’s a choice?”

“Up to this point.” He approached Simar and stopped a few inches from his face. “If you get inside, there’s no turning back. You’re all the way in.” He paused. “That is if you pass the test.”

Simar laughed. “How hard can the test be?”

Francis hesitated.

Simar stared at him, dumbfounded. He had no reason to doubt Francis and trusted him with his life. If he said the blue sky were yellow, he’d believe him over his own eyes. But why was he being reluctant?

“Do you even want me to get in?”

Francis sighed. “I don’t want to hold you back. But I also don’t want you to regret your decision.”

“Why would I regret it?”

“This world, It’s not all fun and games.”

“You’re still having fun.”

Francis laughed. “Bro . . . I’m not the one trying to be an intelligence agent slash commando.”

Simar snorted. It was true. Francis was his own thing. He was a driver. A pilot. Navigator. Behind the wheel he was a well oiled machine with jet fuel in his veins and ice in his blood. A precise, one man wrecking crew who could get anybody in and out of anywhere. Some of the higher ups had him on speed dial as their personal escort. That was the best and worst part about him. He kept to himself and stayed in his lane. No questions. Fully trusted by everybody. Reliable. Any questions Simar had, he didn’t really have much to say. Even if he did, he kept his mouth shut. Simar respected that. And Francis respected him for not pushing him. Secrets were meant to be secrets, even from buddies.

Though Simar was confident he’d unlock all the secrets eventually. And this was the moment. A once in a lifetime opportunity.

He’d never felt more ready to commit to anything.

Simar took in a deep breath and stepped forward. “Don’t worry about keeping me safe. This is the only thing I want.”

Francis nodded. “Alright then.” He approached the side door of the Sedan, opened it wide and motioned for Simar to get in. “It’s gonna leave soon. Better get in.”

Simar stopped at the door and turned to Francis. “See you on the other side.”

Francis put up his hand. “You’re phone and glasses . . . “

Simar obliged and got in the truck.

Francis held onto the door with both hands, ready to close it softly. “Just do exactly as you’re told and you’ll do fine.”

Simar nodded. “Do you even know what I have to do?”

“If I didn’t think you could, I wouldn’t have let you in.”

Great. What a way to be vague. But it did make him feel a bit better.

“By the way . . .” Francis leaned in past the door. “The message I got. It was from Ferez.”

Simar perked up. “Seriously?”

Francis nodded and closed the door. He tapped the window and gave a thumbs up. The vehicle turned on and accelerated towards the shutter door.

Simar gulped and stared at Francis. Goosebumps stood on the back of his neck. What the hell had just happened?

Did he just get to skip multiple levels?

Was it this easy?

There was a hiss—gas releasing. Dizziness overtook him. Darkness.

Chapter Two

Vira stood at the edge of the rose flower garden, staring at her company’s headquarters, Regenesis Inc. The three building complex—two DNA shaped structures curving towards a central bio-ceramic dome, still looked as amazing as her first day on the job. Nearing the end of her morning thirty minute walk, she took one last look and made her way downhill towards the entrance.

She was almost to the gates when a voice rang out behind her. “Oi Vira, hold up . . .”

Vira turned around and smiled, waited as Oscar walked briskly towards her. “Sup. What are you in a rush for?”

He was panting as he caught up next to her. “Carla . . . just got selected.”

“Oh. That’s nice. Congratulations.”

They continued walking.

“Thanks.” He took a deep breath. “Though I’m not sure if it increases my odds. I was borderline quitting.“

There was no point in telling him the truth. Being the significant other of a an LEI wouldn’t increase his odds. His chances were borderline. Maybe he should have waited before proposing to her.

Vira shrugged. “Might as well try it out.”

He chuckled but then looked away, despair in his eyes.

“So how is she taking it?” Vira said as they entered the lobby.

“Beyond excited. Everyone was over last night, I don’t know if you got the message—“

Vira looked at him with a regretful look. “I really had to be somewhere.”

“It’s alright. I shouldn’t have sent it in the group chat, had a feeling you wouldn’t see it.” He then tapped her on the arm. “I’ll do a personal invite next time. Carla’s been looking forward to meeting you‚ still waiting on that dinner invite.”

Vira fake laughed. “One day . . . “

“I’ll just have to force you to come live near us.” He stopped. “How do you do it, everyday, travelling all the way here?”

Vira shrugged.

He looked around behind her and pointed out outside. “It’s so quiet and beautiful down here, I don’t understand why you don’t move here?“

“Don’t worry about it.” Vira stared over the hill towards the villages. Apartments, houses, parks, clinics, supermarkets, warehouses—anything people had voted for, all provided. All delivered. All personalized. No need to ever step outside the borders of Regenesis. And some never did.

Personalization her ass, they were all personalized to the same beliefs. How could any of them claim to be independent when their algorithmically dictated lives all led towards the same goal—to get into the LEI shortlist and live forever.

Vira glanced at the camera on his glasses. Always on. Always recording. It wasn’t even a thought to him at this point. Like breathing. That was what made it so much worse. He was a rag doll without knowing it. The sensors were the strings and the puppet master was the Longevity AI. It wasn’t malicious intent—they were willingly giving up their biodata. But how couldn’t these people realize their whole self worth was based on their perceived worthiness to their non living overlord.

They stopped at the double glass door as the security system scanned their id’s and faces and then entered the left helix shaped building.

As they took the stairs, Vira turned to him. “Why do you care so much?”

Oscar fidgeted and began massaging his ear lobe. “I don’t know . . . When was the last time you showed up to anything.”

Was he trying to mask his heart rate? Vira shook her head. “I told you, I got a lot of stuff going on right now.”

“You’ve been using that same excuse for so long.”

Vira rolled her eyes. “Just let it go will you?”

“What are you always so busy with?”

“You’re being nosy for no reason. It’s my grandma and stuff—“

“You can bring her over whenever.”

“I’ll take that into consideration. She won’t.”

“We can come meet her. We’ve never been to your area before.”

Vira snorted. “I’ll think—“ Vira’s glasses pinged and an urgent message appeared in front of her. Good morning Vira. You’ve been requested to meet with Marshall in room 5307 at 9:10 AM. Please confirm with a reply.

Vira accepted the message. She then looked at Oscar. “Go on ahead, I got a meeting right now. Vira took one step away. “I’ll think about what you said. I should be free in a couple of weeks.”

He shook his head in disbelief and looked at her. “No you won’t.” He then turned serious and leaned in closer to her. “I’m trying to help you. You can’t just expect to keep doing things your own way. It’s not gonna work, It never has.”

Vira narrowed her eyes. “What are you trying to get at?”

He tapped his temple and slightly shook his head. He then walked away. “Better catch your meeting.”

The elevator opened into the fifth floor executive suite. Vira took a seat on the lone red leather couch. The surrounding walls were either transparent or engulfed with ivy and decorative moss. The only sound was from an indoor waterfall, somewhere on the floor. This place seemed perfect for a nap, but her mind was buzzing.

Why this sudden meeting? And what was Oscar going on about? Were the two related? If only the idiot was unshackled, he could have told her something useful.

At exactly 9:10, the vine covered double door to her left opened and a man in a dark blue suit and matching smart glasses stopped in front of her. Vira scrambled to put on her glasses. The words Marshall James hovered above his head.

“Very nice to formally meet you, Vira.”

“Nice to meet you too.” She got up and stared into his lenses, trying to find his eyes through his translucent lens. But he didn’t seem to notice nor care.

He motioned towards the door. “It’s about time. Ten years you’ve been working here. And this is the first time we’re meeting face to face. How does that happen?”

Vira fake laughed. “You should come down into the lab more often.”

“You should come out of the lab more often. I don’t recall seeing you at any of our events the past few years.”

Vira stopped for a second. Seriously? Was everyone except her in on a little secret? Was this guy at the party too? Or was he listening to her previous conversation? What if the glasses were bugged—why this sudden paranoia? She continued walking. “I’m not really a fan of social events.”

“No one doubts that. But you underestimate the power of human connection and teamwork.” He leaned closer turned his head to her. “It can alter trajectories.”

“So I’ve been told.”

Marshall turned around a corner. “Just through here.”

Chapter Four

Vira sat in the corner seat of the skytrain, resting her head against the wall. She'd been staring at the same first line of her virtual lab notebook. Nothing was landing. She waved it away and looked out the window. How the hell was she gonna spend the next thirty minutes? Stare at the cloudy white sky? It was not even noon yet.

It was that stupid meeting. Two days in and she couldn’t think of anything else. Books, movies, running . . . Even heading back to the lab at work. Thought it might help clear her head—totally a bad idea. Just arriving at the building almost gave her a panic attack.

Now there was only one place left to go.

She sighed and looked up at the overcrowded carriage. Quiet. Most people plugged in. A couple of rebels next to each were absorbed with their physical books. Vira wondered what they were reading. If they just moved the books a little higher, her automated program could capture the titles and maybe send new fresh copies to her place if the books aligned with her interests.

The train stopped. New people entered and many left. A man in a sweatsuit who had been standing the whole time stepped closer to her. He had an air filter face mask and sunglasses and seemed to be staring directly at her.

Vira shifted in her seat, feeling hot and gross. Something was off about him. The way he was just looking straight in her direction, not even trying to hide it. Did they get on the same stop together?

Not wanting to get him suspicious by staring back, she unhid the tracker program and widened the lens to the entire carriage. Then she updated the time stamp to when she first got on. Any voice or face or clothing or whatever else the algo matched—whoever got on the train at the same time as her, especially this man, she’d find out.

Eight matches. All highlighted in a light green hue. A woman in the back left corner, face not visible. A couple standing at the center having an intense conversation with one another. A group of teenagers by the door, skipping school. And the last one, the sweatsuit man.

A chill went down her spine and she looked out the window. It could be a coincidence but she couldn’t take any chances. She was only five stops in and the skytrain was just out the city passing over the river bridge. Up next, after a half a mile of forest was the warehouse and manufacturing district, with her favourite rallycross and off-roading course bordering around it. Most, if not all, of these matches should have left by then.

Seven stops to go.

She decided to mostly stare out the window and gave a quick glance during each stop. The number of people reduced significantly as the final three stops approached but that guy was still there, still standing right next to her.

Third last stop. No one was standing anymore. An empty seat to her left. As the doors closed, sweatsuit man finally took that seat. Vira inhaled like she’d just come up from underwater. Even if this guy was random, where she was headed right now, chance was not something to be taken for granted.

As the second last stop approached, Vira gathered her bag, stood up and waited by her seat.

The guy didn’t stir.

The door opened.

Vira held still for four seconds, pretending to look out the window.

The door dinged.

Vira sprang up to the door out onto the platform. A security robot stood at the back, next to the stairs. She walked towards it and glanced back.

The sweatshirt guy was standing sideways beside the closed door, staring at her.

Vira froze, her eyes fixed onto him. The man leaned back against the side wall and looked away.

As the train took off, Vira continued staring at the cabin. There was a split second where the guy had a chance to follow her out. But he stayed behind. Did he fail to make it out? What was his intention? He seemed more than a random creep looking to get a hard-on.

She saved his details and hopped onto the next train. If he ever came near her vicinity again, or anyone matching close to him, she’d find out right away.

The station at the final stop was empty. No familiar bodies nor faces. Vira got out alone and making sure no one was following, took a detour to the back parkade and booked a cab to the outskirts of the warehouse district. The car stopped in front of the rallycross track entrance.

Vira got out and looked at the straight road behind. No vehicles inbound. She took a deep breath and stretched her arms. Whoever that was, at least they wouldn’t follow her in here.

She changed into her driving suit and got into her Pro 4 truck waiting for her in the rented garage vault. Then hit up an all dirt warm up track. For a moment her attention drifted past the dirt loop to the empty backcountry beyond it. There was nothing like the familiar smell of dust and high octane gas and the sounds of the engine and crunching earth. Miles and miles of empty tamed wilderness, she could drive until her mind went empty or the tank.

Vira turned around the corner and began picking up speed when she noticed the operator waving his arms in the safety post. He made a phone call signal with his hands. Vira gave him a thumbs up, then looped back around and exited into the off road pathway.

Backcountry it was. The groomed dirt changed into a bumpy patchwork of gravel and dirt and then hardpan.

Vira eased off the pedal as she took her phone out from the glovebox, turned it on and redialed the last missed call.

Zorina Arleth answered on the other end. “So sorry about this Vira—”

The landscape settled into a rhythm of bumps and tire noise. Up ahead, the terrain turned rougher and rockier, ridges with thick bushes and low shrubs ready to claw her vehicle apart. She circled around for now with the flatter land.

“You think they know about this lab?” Zorina said.

“It’s not possible.” Vira lifted her head up, trying to figure out which direction to go. She slowed down below thirty kilometres. “Right?”

“Can’t discount them for anything.” There was a pause. “Send me a recording of the person. I’ll get our new client to take a look. They’re experts at this kind of stuff.” She snorted. “Who knows, maybe it’s test by them.”

I’ll say something I’m not supposed to, meet someone I’m not supposed to, someone might recognize me and blurt something out they’re not supposed to— I mean there’s so many ways to mess this up. And when do, then what?” Her eyes got watery and she kicked her feet in the dirt. “I just needed a few more days . . . Why do they have to do this?”

Nothing but dust and bumps.

Her speed wasn’t enough to make the jump over the dark, rocky ditch and she spun into a stop with the back of the truck just a foot from the mouth.

One foot.

She had her answer.
